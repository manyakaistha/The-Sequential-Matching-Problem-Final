## What the negative result changes

Round five tested the frozen population control against shipped greedy on 430 independent seeds and six correlated families per seed. The paired difference was +0.000775 MSMI/100, registered 98.75% interval [-0.027215, +0.028766]. It did not establish superiority, inferiority or equivalence, and excluded the planned +0.05 effect. That is strong reason to reject a large improvement claim for that unchanged control; it is not evidence that every predictor or dynamic policy is useless. Round four already tested exact truncated-uniform response learning and marginal acceptance shrinkage. On 32 common-example seed clusters all eight terminal proper-score contrasts were inconclusive; the exact-response refinement was very small and acceptance shrinkage did not improve terminal point loss over exact response learning. A new learner needs a distinct mechanism and its own nonlearning parent, rather than another larger rerun of these scores.

Read evidence: `kit.py`, `research4/candidates.py`, round-five final report, round-four final report/mechanisms/prediction review/literature review, and round-three prospective snapshot contract, prediction audit and available-feedback theorem. These are local evidence, distinct from the literature cited below.

## Source audit: where new signal could exist

**Exact source facts.** The generator draws each soft category uniformly; the actor acceptance bias is N(0, 0.7²), second-intention bias N(0, 0.5²), and fixed response rate Uniform[0.55, 0.98]. There is no explicitly generated demographic-to-actor-bias or demographic-to-soft-category mechanism. Initial completion/missingness is not a preference class. First-stage acceptance uses relationship goal, pace, lifestyle and conversations; second-stage positivity uses goal. Emotional availability, space for relationship and relocation have zero coefficient in this simulator. Shipped greedy counts observed equality in all seven soft fields. It therefore combines useful and nuisance equality, but replacing that with population scoring alone did not earn a round-five improvement. IDs must serve only as stable identifiers or ties, never as PRNG clues.

**Mathematical interpretation, with assumptions.** Under the intended independent population draws, an unobserved soft value has its category prior even after observing demographics and other soft fields, unless public outcome history provides evidence. This is not a theorem about perfect independence of finite deterministic pseudorandom streams. No algorithm may reconstruct the generating stream. Profile autoencoders, demographic clustering and masked-feature prediction cannot manufacture unconditional soft-value information. Population actor priors are also independent of age/gender/zone; fitting demographic success effects from sparse observations risks learning selection artifacts.

**Public outcome history can be useful.** Fixed response rate is reused at introduction and second submission. First acceptance bias and second positivity bias persist across partners. A fresh shared Gaussian dyadic effect enters both reciprocal acceptance and second positivity within an introduction. Current public histories contain responding yes/no and explicit no-response, date outcomes, second outcomes, and retirement/pause events. Their selection into late stages matters. A past mutual acceptance changes the posterior of that introduction's shared effect; treating its later second outcomes as independent of first outcomes is an approximation. Public histories of retired members can inform population/regime estimates; surviving available members are a selected cohort, and a second-stage no remains valuable evidence about a survivor's second bias.

**Timing matters for modeling scope.** The preserved available-feedback lemma proves that a legal available endpoint has no unresolved event from its own previous introductions. A new actor scorer must not add a pending-survival correction to resolved own histories. Pending survival remains relevant to unavailable endpoints, global regime filters and continuation models. Not-yet-observed feedback is never a negative. Exits do not cancel scheduled outcomes, and a positive pause retires endpoints regardless of qualification. A public unavailable flag alone does not identify its cause or release day.

**Public regime evidence is limited but legitimate.** Sparse geography is visible in zone support. A recorded date lag greater than 21 days excludes ordinary delay support, although smaller lags do not establish ordinary status. Preference-shift and drift labels are not visible in profiles. Their posterior can use only feedback and compatible observation likelihoods. A drift intercept after day 35 need not change edge ranking in the same way it changes probabilities; intercept estimation alone is therefore not sufficient decision value.

## Shared data and testing contract

Keep a common prospective prediction cohort separate from changed-policy reward runs. Save the whole JSON observation, phase, day, memory, all legal candidate edges and actual scored forecasts before the action, the chosen matching, supported stochastic decision probabilities, source/configuration hash, stage projections and uncertainty. Preserve post-action outcomes in a separate label table. Existing auxiliary snapshot forecasts are not deployed candidate scores; recompute named models from the saved assignment-time public snapshot and public history only.

Train population parameters across designated training seeds. Validate and tune only on designated development seeds, including whole-seed inner folds; six families of one seed remain together. Select candidates on independent selection seeds and freeze code, preprocessing and parameters before untouched confirmation. Do not designate any examined seed untouched again. Round-five worlds are already examined; this plan's conservative default uses their published summary to guide questions, with new training/development partitions for fitting. Any reuse of old public trajectories for training requires an explicit registry entry and cannot support a fresh confirmatory claim.

Define five nested unconditional outcomes: both first respond; mutual acceptance; date happens; both positive second submissions/positive pause; deadline-qualified reward. Also evaluate both second submissions separately so missing response and negative intention are distinguishable. Conditional-stage likelihoods use the correct eligible denominator. Qualification is date within assignment+30 and both positive intentions within date+3. Full day-100 follow-up resolves the supported day-59 assignments; if a shorter data collection is ever used, unresolved outcomes are censored, not negatives. Use exact public date-cancellation and deadline calculations where applicable, not learned constants. Delay models condition on assignment day and publicly supported delay regime. Preserve shared latent effects when calculating joint terminal probability; do not multiply independently fitted marginal sigmoids or substitute sigmoid(expected features) without a measured error audit.

Report log loss, Brier, joint calibration, terminal calibration and stage-conditional calibration on identical prospective examples. Report assignment-pooled and scenario/seed-balanced statistics separately, clustered intervals and bin support counts. Fix reliability bins before seeing evaluation outputs; degenerate bins receive uncertainty limitations. Slices: missing useful fields, actor history size, time, geographic thickness and all six families. Family labels may be used for offline reporting, never as features. AUC is supplementary. Check prediction transport on each changed-policy cohort, without declaring different-cohort proper losses a paired model comparison.

Every proposed model must first pass likelihood/label tests on constructed states, then common-cohort prediction tests, then terminal policy reward tests with the other modules fixed. Dynamic methods need their own exact-small-model and end-to-end reward tests even if their probabilistic predictor is unchanged. A calibration gain is neither required proof of an allocation gain nor sufficient proof of reward superiority. Retain all failed or inconclusive contrasts.

## Six specific methods worth falsifying

### L1: selection-aware reciprocal actor inference

**Hypothesis.** Correctly using complete reciprocal first and second histories creates more useful terminal ranking than the earlier one-actor first-response shrinkage, especially among returners. The alternative explanation is that the histories are too sparse, and high-second-propensity actors retire before personalized learning can help.

**Objective and implementation.** Build a bounded discrete-posterior or quadrature message model for response rate, persistent acceptance bias and persistent second bias. Integrate a separate shared effect for each historical introduction, conditioned jointly on both observed first responses, observed date and both observed second outcomes. Keep the response-rate factorization exact where its assumptions apply. Missing soft equality remains enumerated or integrated under its public-history posterior. Start with fixed known population priors; offline empirical-Bayes prior fitting is a separate ablation, not silently included. A current never-repeat dyad receives a fresh shared effect, not the inferred past effect. Cache actor sufficient-history signatures and cap quadrature/message iterations.

**Inputs and labels.** Public observations/history only. Labels include explicit first and second response flags, responding yes/no and observed dates; absent stages are structural absence. A pause event is a deterministic summary of the two positive seconds and must not be counted as independent evidence. Query answers visible by the present invocation may refine current posterior beliefs about earlier compatibility, but retrospective prediction audits retain the historical prediction snapshot.

**Controls.** Same FIFO queries and same greedy allocation: population prior; exact-response-only; preserved marginal acceptance; joint first-history inference without second-bias updates; full reciprocal plus second-history inference. The last two isolate joint conditioning and second-bias learning. Freeze regime mixture initially so the effect cannot be attributed to L2.

**Cheap falsifiers.** Numerical likelihood normalization and stage nesting; no influence from future fields/events; one second yes/no moves the second-bias posterior in the expected direction when all other latent variables are known; exact response moments agree with the existing rational reference; high quadrature resolution agrees within a registered tolerance on constructed histories. Quantify posterior approximation error against exhaustive tiny histories. Fail promotion if common-example terminal proper score/ranking diagnostics do not improve, or available repeat endpoints with informative second histories are too few to plausibly supply the target reward gain.

**Resources and failure risk.** Target at most 15 nodes per one-dimensional latent and a small fixed number of message passes, with a timed fallback to the population score. Naive joint enumeration over all actors is excluded. Joint graph inference remains approximate, and replacing messages by marginal products can recreate the earlier error. This is a simulator-specific inference refinement of established Bayesian methods, not a broadly new algorithm.

### L2: public-history regime filtering and robust mixtures

**Hypothesis.** Episode-level posterior coupling uses more information than independently mixing regimes per trial, but only changes decisions where regimes disagree. The competing hypothesis is that history arrives too late or the same matching is preferred under every plausible regime.

**Objective and implementation.** Maintain a small posterior over public mechanism models for ordinary, shifted, drifting and delayed combinations. The initial mixture is fixed before evaluation. Update each completed introduction once with its joint likelihood, integrating actor effects and missing preferences. Use partial-observation likelihoods for globally pending cases only if correctly implemented; otherwise update at complete resolution. Hold out actor histories in checks to expose confounding of personal dislike with regime shift. Test a simple fixed day-35 drift mechanism against a generic bounded change-point filter only if the latter adds decision value. A family label is never an input.

**Controls.** Fixed correct population mixture; public-support-only mixture update; full outcome-history mixture; conservative posterior blend with fixed mixture. Keep FIFO queries, scorer form and allocator unchanged. Also report an offline known-regime diagnostic on already examined development states; do not deploy it or regard it as a terminal bound.

**Falsifiers.** Constructed public support eliminates incompatible delay regimes; unchanged histories yield unchanged posterior; no duplicate event update; synthetic known likelihood examples validate Bayes updates. Register number/fraction of invocations where regime-informed matching differs from the fixed mixture. Reject if prediction improvement consists only of common scale changes with unchanged ranking or if known-regime current-edge diagnostics show negligible action advantage.

**Resources and risks.** Five or fewer mechanism hypotheses, cached likelihoods and bounded event history; runtime work should be linear in new resolved history plus edge scoring. Hard-to-identify shifted coefficients and actor selection can make an overconfident regime filter harmful. A robust blend is useful only if it improves the registered contrast. Adams and MacKay develop Bayesian online change-point inference; their conditional-independence model does not automatically fit this selected, reciprocal history. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)

### L3: coherent offline funnel residual with aggressive shrinkage

**Hypothesis.** A small train-only residual correction for selection and history-size effects outperforms both a fixed public mechanism model and an unconstrained terminal-only learner. The null is that residuals are mostly sampling noise and the simulator mechanisms already specify the predictable part.

**Objective and implementation.** Fit a small regularized model for unknown actor/history-conditioned acceptance and second-stage quantities, with public-mechanism probabilities as offsets or structured priors. Candidate features are useful observed equality, integrated missingness, resolved history counts, day and public component summaries. Do not introduce demographic latent classes unless a train-only conditional analysis supports them. Compare ridge logistic and one shallow bounded nonlinear model only; avoid an unlimited architecture search. Carry coherent nested probabilities and shared-stage dependence through the terminal calculation. A plain terminal-only ridge is a negative control, not a coherent funnel model.

**Controls.** Fixed public model; L1 with fixed priors; train-fitted population-only intercepts; train-fitted history residual; terminal-only ridge. All use FIFO and the same allocation. Offline fitting versus online updating is an explicit factorial toggle, not bundled with querying.

**Falsifiers.** Improvement must hold on independent seed validation, with train-only regularization selection, and not only assignment-weighted aggregate losses. Report calibration and terminal ranking among legal alternatives, with label coverage acknowledged. Compare per-seed and per-history slices with uncertainty. Failure to transport to candidate-selected examples or lack of policy-reward improvement rejects deployment even if baseline-selected prediction losses improve.

**Resources and risks.** At most tens of coefficients or a depth-limited tree ensemble, train offline and export a compact immutable artifact; inference can be O(E) after history summaries. Late-stage selection, sparse positives, surviving-actor bias and public graph features can create spurious associations. No training label may use hidden truth. Date cancellation and uniform feedback deadlines should remain fixed exact components. The selective-labels literature motivates the coverage warning; its proposed evaluation mechanism is not automatically applicable here. [Original author-hosted paper](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/kdd17-selective.pdf)

### L4: component continuation learning and conservative distillation

**Hypothesis.** Public graph structure and endpoint return dynamics yield more decision value than another marginal edge predictor. A short component planner can improve total qualified reward by reserving an endpoint or avoiding unproductive long occupancy; the alternative is that immediate maximal allocation is already best in most reachable small components.

**Objective and implementation.** In fully specified tiny public-state models, solve exact finite-horizon control with waits, busy transitions, nonrepeat dyads and pauses. Then train a small observation-only value residual on public rollout returns from designated development states, using no hidden truth features or labels. Rank a bounded set of legal local matchings by immediate expected qualification plus approximate joint continuation. A teacher planner must use only observable histories and public population transitions for deployable distillation. A privileged-state teacher may be studied solely as a separately labeled headroom diagnostic; its labels cannot train this deployable policy under the user's truth restriction.

**Controls.** Same fixed predictor with immediate greedy; same predictor with current maximum-weight matching; explicit hand-coded scarce-partner reservation; learned component planner; distilled small residual. Component continuation must include a joint term or explicit state model, since two endpoint values need not add independently. General-graph matching must handle odd cycles legally; node penalties alone are not the matching polytope.

**Falsifiers.** On tiny held-out models, learned/distilled action value reproduces exact solutions within a registered regret tolerance; a counterexample where endpoint prices fail is required. On simulator states, prediction-error weighting cannot replace reward testing. Split calibration of busy duration, probability of returning before action day 60, future usable degree and retirement; distinguish returns from qualification. Waiting after the final action has no future introduction value. Reject if planners only improve simulated internal value, or added reservation loses introductions without a measured quality/continuation gain.

**Resources and risks.** Exact solve only small components (for example at most eight endpoints) and a fixed action/sample budget. Large components use legal fallback. Offline training CPU budget is separate from bounded inference; sample counts, tree depth and solver timeout freeze before confirmation. Model-based RL is the appropriate limited formulation; unbounded model-free RL has huge state/action spaces and rare terminal labels. Posterior-sampling RL supplies established exploration ideas, but its finite fully observed MDP regret theorem does not transfer to this hidden-actor graph. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0940)

### L5: uncertainty-gated decision changes and structural representations

**Hypothesis.** Estimation noise harms allocation enough that changing greedy only at credible decision margins beats unconditional learned-score reordering. Graph structure may help choose where a prediction matters; reconstructing arbitrary persona features will not.

**Objective and implementation.** Consider a bounded set of legal matchings: shipped greedy, public-score greedy, and exact or component-local matching. Compute their posterior expected value differences and uncertainty on the same state. Use a shrinkage or lower-confidence rule to retain the baseline unless a registered margin is cleared. The decision margin must evaluate the entire matching, not independent edge intervals, and a dynamic version includes the same continuation estimate in every alternative. Start with analytic degree, component size, bridges, scarce-neighbor counts and alternative matching gaps before fitting any graph representation. An unsupervised component signature is allowed only if its train-only clusters predict different allocation regret beyond these simple controls.

**Controls.** Baseline; unconditional public/learned ranking; a fixed convex score blend; uncertainty gate with the same estimator; analytic graph summaries versus optional learned representation. Same queries and history updates. A blend is not merely adding incomparable baseline equality counts to probabilities: standardize/define its ordering on training data and freeze it.

**Falsifiers.** Test null histories where all posterior scores are exchangeable; gate should not create confident arbitrary differences. Report disagreement frequency, calibrated value-margin intervals on covered prospective comparisons and changed-action reward. Reject if gains disappear under alternative legal tie ordering or if the representation predicts market labels but not allocation regret. Calibration of score uncertainty is itself a hypothesis; credible intervals from a misspecified posterior are not automatic confidence guarantees.

**Resources and risks.** At most a fixed few matchings per state and a bounded posterior sample count; compact summaries and model artifact. Decision-focused loss is an established approach to optimize decision error rather than generic prediction error, but full matching costs are not observed for unchosen edges. SPO-style full-cost training is therefore unavailable without new coverage or an explicitly model-dependent pseudo-target. Such targets are not ground truth or a proof of value. [Original SPO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005)

### L6: bounded exploration with demonstrable feedback reuse

**Hypothesis.** A small early exploration budget earns reward only when information affects several remaining decisions through actor or regime updates. The likely failure mode is that occupancy consumes the very horizon needed to benefit from learning, and successful actors retire.

**Objective and implementation.** Before implementing a full bandit, compute a public-model value-of-information diagnostic in exact tiny models: remaining time, possible return time, multiple untried feasible partners, and effects on other actors/regime. Only then consider a restricted early-horizon randomized choice among a few legal near-tied local matchings, with logged exact categorical probability. Sample an actor or episode posterior once for a coherent decision, rather than independently injecting optimism into each edge. Cap exploration cost, stop at a frozen day threshold, and retain a legal exploitation fallback.

**Controls.** Deterministic posterior mean; random tie-breaking with identical candidate set; Thompson-style posterior choice; same random schedule with learning frozen; acquisition of decision-changing useful fields without outcome exploration. The frozen-learning contrast isolates information benefit from beneficial diversification or keyed random outcome differences. Test no-reuse/no-return tiny models where exploration value should be zero or negative.

**Falsifiers.** Require enough remaining public-model reuse and timely feedback before exploration is allowed. Register decisions actually changed by the acquired information, occupancy cost, repeated actor opportunities and affected later alternatives. A favorable result versus deterministic ranking without an advantage over identical frozen-learning randomization does not establish a learning mechanism.

**Resources and risks.** A few posterior samples and a small supported action menu; no general introduction-level independent propensity fiction for the whole matching. Single-introduction values are correlated through occupied endpoints, and rare positives yield slow feedback. Thompson sampling is established; its general tutorial describes information sharing across actions, but supplies no guaranteed win for this simulator. [Original-author tutorial](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02038)

## Suitability of all requested learning paradigms

| Paradigm | Appropriate use here | Main requirement/risk | Initial decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervised | Small coherent funnel residual, history-conditioned return model | Assignment-time snapshots; selected labels and rare positives | Conditional implementation, L3/L4 |
| Unsupervised | Public component signatures, preference-regime likelihood structure | Must add decision value beyond exact simple graph summaries | Cheap structural audit only, L5 |
| Self-supervised | Predict public future availability/event history or compress history | Masks of independent soft fields provide no unconditional signal; future labels must not become inputs | Reject persona autoencoder; allow later history compression only after value evidence |
| Online | Joint actor posterior and bounded regime filter | Resolved available-actor feedback; pending global evidence; strong shrinkage | L1/L2 |
| Offline | Fit small priors/residuals from public development trajectories | Whole-seed folds; train-only tuning; frozen exported artifact | L3 |
| Contextual bandit | Restricted early near-tie action menu with feedback reuse | Matching interference, delayed feedback, logged supported probabilities | L6 only after exact VOI gate |
| Reinforcement learning | Small-component finite-horizon controller | Belief-state approximation; rare rewards and enormous global matching action space | L4; defer global model-free RL |
| Offline policy learning | Local supported stochastic alternatives, or supervised policy distillation from public-model teacher | Deterministic old logs have no coverage for most changed matchings | Reject unsupported IPS/DR claims |
| Model-based planning | Public exact delay/retirement mechanisms plus estimated actor/regime transitions | Approximation bias, continuation dependence, inference budget | L4 priority after headroom gate |
| Hybrid | Useful soft acquisition + shrunk actor/regime score + exact legal local allocation | Factorial controls needed; bundling can conceal losses | Combine only separately earned components |

Historical deterministic logs permit factual prediction audits and descriptive history analysis. They do not identify the value of most alternative full matchings. Inverse propensity and doubly robust evaluation require supported action probabilities; doubly robust estimation does not cure zero support. New randomized local menus can support a narrow one-step estimand, but neither marginal edge propensities nor a one-step bandit estimator identify unrestricted full-horizon policy value under changing occupancy. Use simulator paired policy experiments as the final evaluator. [Original doubly robust policy paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601)

## Implementation order and stop conditions

These six methods are a gated backlog, not six automatically admitted candidates. The critic objects that six model proposals plus structural proposals would exceed a useful fixed screen. Resolution: admit at most **two learning representatives** in the first broad policy screen, with the director choosing which pass the public-information, likelihood and headroom gates. The defaults are L1 and a simple L4 continuation control; L2 can replace one if its action-disagreement gate is stronger. L3/L5/L6 remain deferred unless a recorded Stage-A decision justifies substitution or a later separately registered wave. Train all residuals and margin thresholds before selection, cap feature/count grids and prohibit per-seed tuning.

1. Audit public-state bottlenecks and exact tiny continuation models. Quantify ranking disagreement, repeat-history support and known-parameter/known-regime diagnostic headroom on designated development states. These are diagnostics, not attainable bounds.
2. Implement L1 and L2 only if their respective information/headroom gates justify them; compare prediction quality on identical prospective examples. Implement a simpler public-mechanism/structural control alongside them.
3. Prefer L4's hand-coded reservation/exact-local control before training continuation values. Keep prediction fixed to identify allocation effects. Train L3 only if residual diagnostic evidence shows coherent predictable error.
4. Add L5 as a cheap robustness control for a supported scorer/controller. Implement L6 only after exact reusable-information cases show potential and simulated exploration costs are tolerable.
5. Combine only components that earn their own focused contrast. Require developer case, critic alternative explanation, statistician experiment review and separate implementation review before independent selection. Freeze once; final sample size and multiplicity come from the director's registered plan. A null result stops that candidate, not a request for endless fresh tests.

Possible new contribution is a simulator-specific reciprocal survivor-history estimator or a component continuation counterexample/controller, with verified reward evidence still needed. The statistical learning, posterior sampling, change-point detection, decision-focused learning and shrinkage ideas cited here are established. Broad novelty remains unestablished. No method in this document is claimed superior, optimal, calibrated or production-ready.

## Independent planning review

The named critic read this proposal and reported no source or protocol blocker. The critic specifically accepted L1's marginal/second-off controls, the public-only L4 teacher and the two-representative screen cap. Unresolved objections remain substantive: available survivor histories may cap L1's useful headroom; regime inference can be weakly identifiable; multi-policy training rows sharing a seed remain correlated and factual coverage does not become counterfactual coverage. Resolution is to perform exact tiny joint-likelihood/posterior approximation-error audits before L1's proper-score gate, retain whole-seed splitting/clustering for every training policy, and require direct registered reward evaluation rather than an offline-policy value claim. This is a planning review, not approval of an unimplemented likelihood, learned model or production policy. The director's independent statistical plan controls alpha, power and final promotion rules.
