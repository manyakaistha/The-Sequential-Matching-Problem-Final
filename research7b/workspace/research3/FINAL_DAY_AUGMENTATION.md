## Proposed source lemma and scope

Hypothesis: any final-action-day legal matching can be augmented by a legal unused edge between unmatched available endpoints without changing any existing qualified outcome, and final qualified count weakly increases pathwise. Independent dyad/day PRNG keys preserve original per-edge draws even when batch order changes. No future assignments, exits or pauses cancel existing scheduled outcomes. Under the intended stochastic mechanism, each such feasible edge with finite logits and positive response propensities has strictly positive expected terminal reward. Any failed byte-identity or original-outcome check falsifies the proposed pathwise statement on that trajectory.

The intended scope requires unique introduction identifiers (no truncated-hash collision); source does not prove hash injectivity over arbitrary adversarial identifiers. It assumes supported full day100 follow-up, no changes to original pairs, disjoint available endpoints and original legality. It does not establish a globally optimal matching, justify early-day augmentation, quantify an attained positive realized gain in every world, or permit a new derived policy into the frozen experiment.

## Source proof after the fixed diagnostic

**Final-day augmentation lemma (collision-free identifiers).** Fix a legal simulator history before the last action's matching phase, a legal matching M, and another unused legal edge e whose endpoints are unmatched by M. Keep M and all earlier actions unchanged, append e and take no subsequent introduction/query actions; collect all supported outcomes through day100. Then every previously assigned introduction has exactly the same outcome events and qualification indicator as without e. The final qualified count increases by the qualification indicator of e, hence weakly increases pathwise.

**Proof.** `advance` first checks batch endpoint disjointness, availability, no repeated dyad and observed hard eligibility. M union{e} satisfies those checks by assumption. Per-edge assignment execution sorts endpoint IDs and calls a fresh local `rand_for(world_seed,a,b,assignment_day)`. Adding another disjoint edge does not change the arguments or local draws for any old dyad/day. The corresponding event identity includes only its dyad/day. Under collision-free introduction IDs, old events are therefore individually identical, even if their positions in the augmented event list shift. Busy-release values assigned to e's endpoints cannot alter the values of already scheduled outcomes: the simulator creates all events at assignment, follow-up reveals them by observation day, and exits/busy/positive pauses do not cancel them. No future actions use the changed availability, so there is no subsequent opportunity cost. Previously pending assignments on other endpoints are unchanged as well. All supported day59 outcomes arrive by day97, inside day100 follow-up. Final scoring sums the introduction-specific qualification indicators over distinct introductions, so the added indicator is the entire difference. ∎

Under the intended stochastic law, conditional on finite actor parameters, q(e)=.78*(r_a r_b)^2*.36*h*E[product of four positive sigmoid factors], with h=1 in ordinary families or671/686 in delayed. Response propensities are positive and h>0. Thus q(e)>0; integrating a posterior over finite parameters retains strict positivity. Consequently the augmentation has strictly positive expected qualified-count gain. This probabilistic positivity uses the stated stochastic interpretation; pathwise preservation itself is a deterministic source property and does not assume independent PRNG streams.

The scalar objective here is qualified count/MSMI100, with fixed arrived-member denominator. Ask cost is unchanged. Latency/coverage/date counts may change. This is a useful final-day dominance correction, not a proof that M union{e} is the optimal final matching: replacing M edges or choosing a different augmentation can do better. Earlier-day augmentation can block better future dyads and has no such general guarantee. An introduction-ID collision would invalidate introduction-specific scoring separation; no universal hash injectivity is asserted.

## Controlled diagnostic results

All24 fixed trajectories retained byte-identical existing events, exact original introduction lists, and every original qualified outcome. There were37 appended zero-score edges and zero extra realized qualified outcomes. Privileged15-point Gaussian quadrature summed their expected qualification probabilities to.159206 qualified outcomes over the24 interventions. The numerical expectation is conditional on true actors/soft features along the examined prefix, not an evaluated deployable score or a measured causal average across an untouched sample. Rare expected events readily yield no realized successes; zero realized improvement does not refute strict-positive expected gain. The corresponding scenario/policy/seed-balanced mean expected gain on this fixed equal-size grid is.003317 MSMI/100 (.159206 divided by24 trajectories and two), while realized mean gain is zero.
