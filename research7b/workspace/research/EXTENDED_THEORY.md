# Audited extension: dynamic costs and terminal probabilities

8 October 2026. Earlier analysis in `THEORY.md` is preserved. This extension separates proven statements, exact calculations, rational toy-MDP solutions, numerical approximations and unvalidated policy hypotheses.

## Simulator audit and information boundary

Source of truth: `kit.py` and `evaluate.py`, read before design. Policies observe only the JSON state and their serialized memory. Observable quantities are member attributes and answered fields, field statuses, availability, assignment day and dyad identities, received events, clarification log, current day and budget. Latent quantities are unanswered truth fields, actor acceptance and second biases, response propensity, the actual exit day, future arrivals, common pair effect and future scheduled events/busy-release times. Public mechanisms are the generator distributions and transition code, including six possible scenario families. An evaluated policy cannot read the episode's family, latent truth or seed from the JSON interface; a fixed prior or posterior mixture is an assumption, not an observation.

The evaluator gives 60 action days (0–59) and 40 follow-up days, with metrics at day 100. Eleven hard fields must be answered; declined fields remain unavailable after any constraints query. Members can have repeated introductions, but dyads cannot repeat. New assignments need current availability; no action cancels an existing pair. Exit is assigned to 12% of generated members in total, uniformly on days 22–60. Conditional on surviving earlier exit opportunities the prior daily hazard is (.12/39)/(1-.12*(d-22)/39), not .12. Arrivals stop by day 20.

Every non-mutual pair is busy for eight days. A mutually accepting pair is busy until date day plus six even if its date is cancelled. All positive second responses cause a pause after both are observed, including late responses or dates too late to qualify. Qualification requires an actual date within 30 days of assignment and both positive seconds within three days of the date. Retirement therefore has a different probability from qualified reward. Explicit no-response is observed at introduction age seven or date age five; before then lack of response is pending information. Date cancellation is a separate boolean event. An introduction without mutual acceptance generates no date or second event, rather than an explicit date cancellation.

## Proven probability and approximation statements

**Joint-probability impossibility.** A joint event probability cannot exceed either marginal. Harmonic fusion at p_u=p_v=.5 gives .5, while independent joint success gives .25. Harmonic fusion is a reciprocal ranking function; it is not a joint Bernoulli probability.

**Shared-effect covariance.** For increasing f,g and iid S,S', 2 Cov(f(S),g(S))=E[(f(S)-f(S'))(g(S)-g(S'))]≥0. Therefore shared-effect mutual acceptance is at least the product of its conditional marginal means. This statement conditions on the other actor/dyad parameters. Selection or mixing heterogeneous dyads can alter unconditional associations; observed pool correlations alone do not validate a latent model.

**Logistic mean-substitution bound.** For finite-variance Z and μ=E[Z], Taylor's theorem with globally bounded second derivative gives |Eσ(Z)-σ(μ)| ≤ Var(Z)/(12√3), since sup|σ''|=1/(6√3). No Jensen direction holds globally because the logistic curve changes curvature. The script exactly enumerates all 16 equality/non-equality configurations for the four missing soft predictors under independent public priors in development and shift. On intercepts {-2,-1,-.25,0,1,2}, the maximum observed plug-in probability error is .020874193. This is an audited grid maximum, not a universal bound; the variance bound applies globally under its stated moments. Known hard constraints do not make missing soft features deterministic.

**Numerical shared-effect example.** Fixed acceptance logits -.25,.55 and second logits .15,.65, with both response rates .765: 64-node Gauss-Hermite integration gives mutual acceptance .287503762 versus independent integrated marginals .276880469. Qualified probability is .011211797 versus a four-marginal independence approximation .009277865. Results at 16,32,64 nodes are recorded for convergence checking; Gaussian integration is numerical and is not called exact enumeration. This chosen-parameter example demonstrates approximation error, not average deployed calibration.

## Exact dynamic benchmark definitions

Five tiny, fully specified finite-horizon MDPs are solved by exhaustive legal matching enumeration and rational Bellman recursion. States include day, busy-release vector, retired subset and used-dyad subset. Arrivals and exits are known deterministic schedules in these benchmarks. Each edge has expected terminal reward q, probability p≥q of jointly retiring both endpoints, busy duration d, and allowed assignment interval. On nonretirement endpoints return after d days. One can couple reward as a subset of retirement with probability q/p conditional on retirement. Outcomes are independent across simultaneously disjoint edges and observed by the next toy step. The expected reward is booked at assignment once. This simplified feedback model differs substantially from the full simulator's latent schedules and actor learning; none of these exact optima solve the full simulator.

Comparators:

- Greedy sorts currently feasible edges by q and takes disjoint positive edges.
- Blossom maximizes current total q using general-graph matching; the script independently verifies every returned current optimum against enumeration.
- Batching waits on even days and uses blossom on odd days.
- Reservation uses edge-specific q minus the difference between tomorrow's static matching value after waiting and its expected value after assigning that edge. It uses the toy's known arrivals, departures, retirement and busy probabilities. It optimizes the sum of these individually adjusted weights; that separability is the challenged approximation.
- Exact DP jointly considers all current matchings and their exact state-dependent future values, including waiting.

| Fully specified instance | Exact DP | Greedy | Blossom | Batching | Reservation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Static greedy trap | 1.2 | .7 | 1.2 | 0 | 1.2 |
| Known future arrivals | 1.6 | .3 | .3 | 1.6 | 1.6 |
| Early exit | .8 | .8 | .8 | 0 | .8 |
| Nonseparable reservation | 1 | 1 | 1 | .9 | .9 |
| Stochastic returning members | .709 | .709 | .709 | .700 | .616 |

Raw JSON includes every edge, schedule, rational expected reward, rational regret and initial action, so these are exactly reproducible outcomes, with no simulator seeds or statistical significance interpretation.

**Nonseparable continuation counterexample.** Day zero offers disjoint AB and CD, each reward .5 and certain joint retirement, expiring after that day. Day one offers AC with reward .9. With all four present tomorrow, continuation value is .9. Removing AB alone loses .9, removing CD alone loses .9, but removing both still loses only .9. Summed individual opportunity costs double-count the same alternative. Individually adjusted edges are negative, so the reservation policy waits and obtains .9, while jointly taking both yields 1. More generally, a matching's exact continuation loss need not be the sum of its edge continuation losses. This proves failure of that reduction; it does not prove all approximate node prices or penalties perform badly empirically.

**Node prices and odd sets.** The degree-only matching LP on a unit-weight triangle allows half of each edge, value 1.5, while every legal matching has value 1. The missing odd-set constraint is x_AB+x_AC+x_BC≤1. General-graph dual accounting therefore includes odd-set multipliers in addition to node prices. Even a correct static blossom dual does not imply dynamically separable continuation value. [Edmonds's weighted polyhedral paper](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69B/jresv69Bn1-2p125_A1b.pdf) is the directly applicable static result.

**Unrestricted time monotonicity fails.** In a separate four-day simplified market, AB always offers immediate .1 and locks A,B for two days without retirement. Independently with probability .5, C becomes available only on day three and AC offers reward 1. Assignment of AB on days zero or one releases A by day three, so its continuation cost is zero. Assignment on day two blocks AC, cost .5. Assignment on day three has no future cost, zero. Constant busy duration thus gives costs 0,0,.5,0. This does not refute the stationary iid one-stop threshold theorem in `THEORY.md`; future arrival windows violate its assumptions. Coupled dyads are not independent rested or restless bandit arms, and no indexability claim follows.

## Information acquisition and complementarity

**Verified edge creation is not generally submodular.** Two otherwise compatible endpoints u,v both need hard bundles. Define F(Q) as current maximum matching value after verifying queried members. F(∅)=F({u})=F({v})=0, F({u,v})=1. The marginal value of v increases after u, violating diminishing returns. An individual-query greedy method with a strict zero-gain stopping rule never discovers this edge. A two-query lookahead can select both. Uncertain hard fields may invalidate the pair; hypothetical compatibility can guide query selection but can never legalize an introduction before all eleven fields are observed.

**Information value must be decision value.** Querying an independent fair coin gives one bit of entropy reduction and zero expected matching-value gain. Under unchanged feasible decisions and reward posteriors, the query's decision value is zero. Under unrestricted future hard acquisition or shared regime learning, immediate infeasibility does not prove dynamic zero value. Positive entropy is not sufficient for an action to have positive terminal-reward value.

**Global connectivity can miss useful expansion.** On five vertices, graph {01} and graph {01,23} both have disconnected components and λ₂=0; maximum matching cardinality increases from one to two. Component-aware structural changes can be useful despite unchanged global algebraic connectivity. Entropy and λ₂ results are counterexamples, not blanket claims against information-theoretic or spectral models.

The applicable literature concerns particular objectives and assumptions. [Krause and Guestrin](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~guestrin/Publications/UAI2005/uai2005.pdf) prove approximation results for their information objectives; their submodularity assumptions must be verified before transfer. [Akbarpour, Li and Oveis Gharan](https://web.stanford.edu/~mohamwad/DynamicMatching.pdf) analyze waiting and departure information in a dynamic market; [Baccara, Lee and Yariv](https://www.lyariv.com/papers/DynamicMatching.pdf) study composition-dependent optimal matching. Their information structures and irreversible matching dynamics differ from this simulator. Prior regularization and proper scoring references are added to `docs/REFERENCES.md`; no cited source guarantees our approximate online calibration.

## Qualified reward and permanent-positive pauses: conditional theorem

**Assumptions.** Fix one of the shipped public families, the set of generated members and a legal policy adapted to its observation/action history. Interpret each newly assigned dyad/day's simulator random stream as fresh independent draws conditional on actor truth and the preassignment history. Follow every action-day assignment until all its supported events have matured. This is the simulator's intended stochastic-transition interpretation; the executable at a fixed seed is a deterministic hash/PRNG computation, for which exact independence is not a theorem. The stated reward identity is not a deterministic fixed-seed ratio.

For a potential assignment e on day t, let I_et indicate the policy assigns it, P_et indicate that assignment produces an actual date and both positive second intentions (hence the permanent pause event), and D_et indicate the date occurs within thirty days and both second responses occur within three days of the date. Qualified reward is Q_et=P_et D_et. Conditional on preassignment history and fixed actors/soft truth, P_et depends on the common Gaussian, responder draws, acceptance draws, date occurrence and second-intention draws. D_et depends only on the independent introduction-response delays, date offset and second-feedback delays. Therefore

E[Q_et | assignment-time history, I_et=1] = c_f E[P_et | assignment-time history, I_et=1],

where c_f=9/25=.36 in every family except delayed, and c_delayed=(9/25)(671/686)=6039/17150≈.352128280. Integrating actor uncertainty preserves this equality because c_f is fixed within the known family. The result does not require pause probability itself to be calibrated by a particular deployed model.

**Theorem.** Write N_Q for the final qualified count, N_P for final positive-pause event count, and N_R for the number of distinct endpoints ever receiving such a permanent positive pause. For every adapted legal policy under these assumptions,

E[N_Q] = c_f E[N_P] = (c_f/2) E[N_R].

**Proof.** A policy's assignment indicator is measurable before that assignment's fresh draws. Conditional expectation gives E[I_et Q_et]=c_f E[I_et P_et]. Sum over the finite set of dyad/day opportunities and apply linearity. Future actions may depend on observed date/feedback delays, including whether prior assignments qualified; this does not change the preassignment conditional identity for any fresh assignment. No assumption that deadline outcomes are independent of the eventual number of assignments or pause events is needed.

For the second equality, every positive-pause assignment locks its endpoints until date day plus six. Both positive second responses, and hence its pause event, are observed by date day plus five. An endpoint therefore cannot receive another legal assignment between its current assignment and the pause. After the pause the public `available` predicate remains false permanently. Positive-pause assignments have disjoint endpoint sets, so N_R=2N_P pathwise after complete follow-up. Apply expectation. ∎

N_R counts the endpoints of recorded `pause_after_mutual_interest` events; it does not count exits or all currently unavailable members. The class's unused `sim.retired` set is not the measurement. Exits do not cancel scheduled simulator outcomes, and legal policies cannot revoke assignments; these shipped mechanisms matter to the proof. Day-59 delayed assignments mature by day97, so official day100 follow-up is sufficient. A truncated feedback window, a process that cancels dates after exits, repeated assignments during the busy interval or feedback-dependent cancellation would require a new analysis.

A policy may change expected qualified reward through its expected number of completed positive pauses; within each fixed family, maximizing either expectation is equivalent under the theorem's assumptions. The identity does not imply equal realized qualification fractions, conditional Binomial labels given N_P, independence between total pauses and deadline marks, or causal identification from descriptive finite-seed trajectories. Pooling families requires E[N_Q]=Σ_f w_f c_f E[N_P|f]; delayed has a slightly different constant. Scoring expected pause alone is proportional to terminal scoring only conditional on a known family and a coherent probability model; a policy's uncertain delayed-family posterior or approximation errors need not preserve a single global ranking constant.

`adaptive_pause_identity_audit` exactly enumerates a reduced rational example. A first disjoint pair certainly pauses; the policy introduces a second certainly pausing pair only if the first qualifies. Its qualification marks are independent with probability c. Despite the endogenous assignment count, E[N_P]=1+c and E[N_Q]=c+c²=c E[N_P]. However E[N_Q/N_P]=c(1+c)/2, which is not c. For standard c=.36, expected reward is306/625=.4896, expected pauses34/25=1.36, and the expected realized ratio153/625=.2448. Thus the tower identity is valid while a stronger expected-ratio claim fails. The toy is explicitly artificial, with no full-simulator rollout or new evaluation seeds.

## Pure FIFO clarification invariance

**Proposition.** Fix the same generated world, initial valid history, member observation order, daily clarification budget and permanent-decline pruning. Two policies whose clarification rule is the same pure FIFO sequence of hard constraints bundles have identical hard-query schedules and costs, regardless of their legal matching decisions. Assume answered hard fields are never erased, every bundle reveals all nondeclined hard fields, and no extra queries consume the shared budget or change queue priority.

**Proof.** Every nondeclined hard-incomplete member is inadmissible for an introduction. It therefore cannot be made busy or positively paused by either matching policy before receiving its hard bundle. Its availability while in the query queue depends only on its fixed arrival/exit process, shared across the policies. At day zero both policies have the same ordered eligible incomplete queue and budget, so select the same FIFO prefix. The same bundles return the same nondeclined hard truth and permanently remove those members from the incomplete queue. Declined members remain pruned. Inductively at each later day, the remaining incomplete queue's membership, relative order and availability are identical, including newly arriving members and exogenous exits. Both select the same prefix again. Constraints cost three each, so schedules and accumulated ask costs agree. ∎

Feedback and matching may alter availability among already verified members, but those members are absent from the hard-incomplete queue. Removing verified members from an intermediate available list does not change the relative order of incomplete members. This proof covers the shipped greedy baseline and the frozen pure-FIFO controls, including `simple_fifo`, provided normal/fallback execution implements that same queue. It explains identical asks structurally, rather than attributing them to fortunate finite-seed randomness. It does not imply identical matching opportunities after availability and used-dyad restrictions, introductions, feedback, coverage or reward.

The proposition does not cover soft-budget fill, hard-field queries that leave a member incomplete, feedback-adaptive prioritization, degree/value queries, changing observation order, later erasure of answered fields, or simulator mechanisms that alter an unassigned incomplete member's availability through other members' assignments. For the selected simple-FIFO versus greedy comparison, any observed gain cannot be explained by a different hard-clarification schedule under the proposition; scoring, allocation/tie order, consequent availability and stochastic outcomes remain possible explanations requiring their own evidence.
