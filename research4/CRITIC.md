## Coordinated clarification

Developer's strongest case: legal same-member constraints+goal asks occur before
matching, so first introductions can exploit answers unavailable to round3
surplus querying. Goal equality positively enters both stages in all regimes.
This is a real public protocol mechanism. Its same-score surplus parent isolates
the complete timing/budget policy intervention.

Strongest opposition: reserving3units displaces a hard bundle when four compete;
queries on newly verified actors may be declined or never decision-changing.
Knowing only one endpoint's goal leaves equality probability unchanged when the
other's goal remains uniformly missing. Canonical acquisition may query unrelated
endpoints instead of a mutually feasible pair. Increased information, improved
score variation and altered matching do not imply reward gain. The comparison
changes both hard throughput and soft timing, so its net reward contrast is causal
for the bundled policy, not either component alone.

Falsifiers: coordinated−surplus reward<=0, no measurable decision changes, or loss
of legal introductions exceeding any conditional-quality benefit. Count displaced
bundles, newly verified soft answers, matching disagreements, positive pauses and
qualification with all zero-event episodes retained. Descriptive chosen-edge qsum
and deadline draws remain alternative explanations; no causal attribution from
trajectory differences alone. A separate future deferred-bundle-only control
would isolate budget displacement, but is not required to change this frozen
candidate set after B.

## Hard verification priority

Strongest case: public age/gender/zone and partial hard failures identify likely
near-term partners before buying a bundle. The FIFO parent holds scoring and
allocation fixed. This can remove unproductive early verification without
claiming unobserved fields are legal.

Strongest opposition: sums of partial completion proxies double-count competing
partners; high degree may crowd the same scarce verified endpoints. Arrivals,
exits, opportunity changes and starvation can reverse current utility. Omitted
smoking/children/structure/schedule priors and correlated scenario likelihood
mean the product is not calibrated hard-feasibility probability. This review
caught an initial use of own-zone frequencies for the uniformly drawn extra
accepted zone; developer corrected it before evaluation. The resulting .0625
unconditional marginal still ignores public zone-support evidence, explicitly
labeled. Exact known age/gender factors do not repair omitted dependencies.

Falsifiers: no gain versus population FIFO, fewer productive verified opportunities,
or low-value starvation/occupancy despite increased introduction counts. Show
verification times and feasible edge counts with denominator definitions. Current
expected degree cannot establish a dynamic scheduling theorem.

## Exact response posterior

Strongest case: replacing moment-matched Beta with the generator's truncated-
uniform posterior is mathematically correct for resolved submission counts under
the specified response sampling interpretation. The available-endpoint theorem
eliminates own pending-feedback censoring. Identical Beta control isolates this
arithmetic change.

Strongest opposition: prior first/second moments already match, and most available
actors have little history. Corrections can be too small to alter ranking and can
change keyed outcomes favorably by chance. The coherent funnel still approximates
missing-feature/post-selection and regime beliefs. Better parameter correctness
is not proof of terminal calibration or sequential reward. Finite worst-cell
posterior errors do not quantify attainable headroom.

Falsifiers: no prospective proper-score gain or action changes on matched cohorts;
reward<=0 versus Beta on serious paired seeds. Report count-cell frequencies,
ranking reversals and repeat exposure. Preserve negative findings rather than
making a larger model to explain away an exact control's failure.

## Acceptance shrinkage

Strongest case: resolved responding yes/no labels can infer a reusable actor
acceptance bias; a known Gaussian prior and retained uncertainty shrink sparse
histories. Population scoring misses this potentially reusable public signal.
Its exact-response parent isolates the acceptance update.

Strongest opposition: the likelihood independently remixes trial regimes and
ignores posterior dependence induced by partner/shared fit, past second labels
and remaining available-actor selection. Current public soft disclosures can
legally inform past fit but do not make this an exact posterior. Replacing a
five-point posterior by a rounded Gaussian moment approximation can distort
nonlinear terminal expectation. Repeated availability selects unsuccessful
actors; learning a lower bias may simply reduce productive repeats. Actor cache
misses increase cost. One successful monotonic toy test is arithmetic sanity,
not model calibration.

Falsifiers: no matched-cohort terminal proper-score improvement, worse history-size
calibration, reward loss versus response_uniform, cache/latency failures, or gains
restricted to favorable deadline draws. Retain reciprocal dependence assessment;
individual yes calibration does not validate joint terminal ranking. No hidden
actor parameters, scenario tags or future disclosures may reach prediction.
