## Reported comparisons and the questions they answer

The detailed narrative and result tables are in `research9/round2/REPORT.md`.

- **FIFO under changing conditions / G vs alternatives (Q1).** Directly addresses whether FIFO asking and greedy allocation retain performance as arrival ordering/process, preference weights, and response behavior change. Reported paired differences vs G: G_arr +0.09896 (95% CI [+0.0401,+0.1579], p=.0010, Holm=.0040); G_lifo +.00260 (CI [-.0440,+.0492], p=.9128); Q6 -.03125 (CI [-.0867,+.0242], p=.2690); G_deg -.06771 (CI [-.1321,-.0033], p=.0394, Holm=.1182); P -.11719 (CI [-.1724,-.0620], p<.0001, Holm=.0002). The positive `G_arr` contrast is specifically arrival-ordered query FIFO, distinct from shipped G's baseline FIFO query ordering. The report attributes this to greater feedback maturation time; however, its stated 40-day follow-up means this causal explanation is not established by these outcome comparisons and should be removed or tested separately.
- **Observable vs simulator vs flat priors (Q2).** Asks whether training-data-derived priors change conclusions. Report says public training data `data/public_01`–`public_10` (2,000 members, day-30 snapshots) produced empirical response/acceptance/date estimates, then compares priors in the 420-cell grid. Reported point outcomes: N3e_emp .5667, N3e_sim .5583, N3e_flat/G .5167; no corrected contrast establishes a gain. PS variants score below G. Treat these as small-study point estimates and uncertainty, not proof of prior equivalence.
- **Adaptation and asking/calibration ablations (Q3).** Tests whether X1 addresses drift with fixed baseline asks; contrasts empirical/simulator priors, no calibration, offset-only/no learning, static PS, and an oracle drift schedule. Reported arm means: G .5833, X1_sim .5139, X1_oracle .4583, X1_emp .4444, X1_nocal_emp .4444, X1_nolearn_emp .4306, PS_emp .4306. This answers whether these particular versions/ablations beat G and isolates components; it does not show general adaptation is impossible.
- **Round1 context.** The Round2 report explicitly clarifies that shipped G already uses FIFO clarification asks; the Round1 `simple_fifo` candidate was below G (paired difference -.042, 95% CI [-.091,+.008]). This prevents conflating a candidate named FIFO with G's FIFO ask ordering.

## Research8 and Research6 context to retain

- `research8/README.md` labels its work reduced-model correctness/development: no simulator win, deployment admission, public model fit, or confirmation. Its fixed 144-case toy grid had 20 strict gains and zero menu-rollout regret only on a sparse four-actor family; external validity is explicitly unestablished. Its constructed falsifiers show greedy tail regret 3/10 and 2/5; static max-weight repairs only the first, while exact belief continuation solves both. Production fidelity gate failed for the approximations; exact core is capped at ≤8 declared actors and is not a 200-member controller.
- `research8/continuation_probe/DECISION.md` describes a 24-cell scheduled-return probe, fully audited but only two independent seed groups. It explicitly cannot establish equivalence, benefit, or impossibility of continuation improvements. It defers rollout and proposes assignment-time public outcome prediction as the next bounded question; `NEXT_EXPERIMENT.md` lays out that design without authorizing fitting or a large simulation.
- `research6/REPORT.md` is the completed pre-registered confirmation: 12,012 episodes / 751 fresh clusters, with untouched Stage E `N3_two` vs G on 571 clusters (6,852 episodes), mean +0.003211 MSMI/100, 99% CI [-0.024483,+0.030904], p=.764559. Superiority was not established; G remains supported default. The report separates development screens from confirmatory evidence and should anchor any claim about confirmation.

## Suggested final-report question framing

1. Does shipped G's FIFO clarification strategy remain robust when arrival order, arrival process, preferences, and response rates change?
2. Is chronological arrival-ordered querying associated with different outcomes across arrival laws, and does that relationship persist after the stated 40-day follow-up is accounted for?
3. Do observable-data priors change rankings or outcomes relative to simulator-informed and flat priors?
4. Does recency-weighted adaptation or directional calibration improve outcomes under drift, and what do the no-calibration/no-learning/oracle ablations reveal?
5. Who waits or remains unserved, and are differences associated more with demographic groups or with missing/declined hard information?
6. How do introductions progress through mutual acceptance, dates, and qualified post-date outcomes, and how well do terminal predictions match realized outcomes?
7. What did earlier confirmatory R6 and reduced R8 work establish, and which findings remain development-only?
