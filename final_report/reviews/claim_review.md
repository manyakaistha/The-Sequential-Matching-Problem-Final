## Submission-critical corrections

1. **Round 2 inference is not seed-cluster inference.** `research9/round2/analyze.py` appends one contrast per `(seed,law)`, uses a normal p-value and an ad hoc critical value, and its Holm implementation omits the cumulative maximum. The independent replication count is 12 seeds, not 192 Q1 policy pairs, 60 Q2 pairs or 72 Q3 pairs. The large apparent Q1 `G_arr` significance must not be imported unchanged. Reanalysis of the existing outcomes at seed-cluster level is appropriate; it is validation, not permission to run new simulations. State the study is exploratory and disclose multiplicity scope.

2. **The published Round 2 prose overclaims superiority and mechanisms.** It calls G superior to all models although its own Q3 corrected p-values exceed .05. X1_emp and X1_nocal_emp have identical aggregate results, so the calibration component has no established outcome benefit; a direct adaptive-versus-static paired contrast is necessary before claiming an improvement. `policies/model.py::_rank` immediately fills a greedy legal matching and has no hold/score-threshold action. Therefore its deficits do not establish that models lose by deliberately withholding pairs. Research8's exact reduced examples explicitly show conditions where waiting helps; "holding back is strictly destructive" is not a theorem of this problem.

3. **The G_arr feedback-cutoff explanation is wrong.** The supplied evaluation and Round 2 runner use 60 decision days followed by 40 feedback-only days. Qualified outcomes are defined by date within 30 days of introduction and both second responses within 3 days of the date, not by feedback arriving before decision day 60. Earlier clarification may change availability and matching trajectories, but no isolating experiment establishes the proposed mechanism. Describe it as a hypothesis, and distinguish decision horizon from outcome follow-up.

4. **The empirical prior is a hybrid, not a fully observable-estimated model.** `fit_priors.py` fits coefficients/moments from public day-30 data but keeps acceptance/shared/second latent SDs at `.65/.45/.5`; it uses fixed initial coefficients, support ranges and fallbacks. Its date denominator includes mutual accepts with dates not yet observed, without a maturity restriction or censoring correction. The date rate `.4762` versus simulator `.78` is a warning, not successful recovery. Absence of a significant empirical-vs-simulator outcome difference is not equivalence or prior invariance. A clean fully observable-only admitted prior remains a gap; the closed final campaign separately supplies public-history training with a failed predictive gate.

5. **Round 2 is not independently audited on the evidence reviewed.** No `certify.py` or `audit.py` is present under `research9/round2`. Its unit parity test covers one seed, four families, 30 decision days and endpoint counts; it does not support 60-day all-family full-observation parity across 12,000+ invocations. `study.py` caps workers at seven but has no monitored aggregate RSS guard, immutable writer, source freeze or seed-freshness certificate. The report's blanket audited/fresh/frozen/resource-compliant claims need qualification. An independent validator of the existing result files can check completeness, identities and consistency without replaying new science.

6. **Closed final campaign is a selection-stage negative result.** `selection.json` explicitly states `8reusedvalidationgroups; selectiononly; noformalpolicyvalueclaim`. The 288 audited cells have exact world/event/reference parity. No candidate beat G's screen point mean, so no candidate qualified under the fixed admission rule. The 96 confirmation groups were not opened. This supports adherence to the stop rule, not confirmatory population superiority, universal inability to improve G, or a definitive negative result for all learned policies. The source FINAL_REPORT's stronger adjectives should not be inherited.

## Metrics, cohorts and diagnostics

- Keep G (supplied greedy allocation + public-list-order FIFO hard asks), Round 1 `simple_fifo` (different scorer/tie ordering), G_arr (arrival-day-ordered asks + G allocation), P and Q6 separate. P/Q6 are frozen prior-round references, not supplied release-kit baselines.
- Show qualified MSMI/100 arrived as the primary metric and completed dates/100 as the principal secondary outcome. Realized outcomes and host-only conditional expected oracle rewards have different roles; oracle values are neither policy inputs nor real-world predictions.
- Do not pool Round 1's 40-cluster confirmation, Round 2's 12-seed exploratory grid, and final campaign's eight reused selection groups into one effect or uncertainty estimate. Different laws/arms and development exposure imply different inferential scopes.
- Round 2's executed union is 20 laws: 16 Q1 laws plus four additional drift laws in Q3. The 22 defined laws also contain W_random/R_slow_second, which are not in the executed campaign. Q1 W_half_shift is a fixed halfway mixture of coefficient vectors, not a temporal midway change.
- Wait times are among members who received an introduction; they exclude unserved/censored members. Pair these with unserved rates. Repeated member-episode rows across policies/laws are not independent unique people.
- "100% unserved with a declined hard field" follows from hard-feasibility rules. Aggregate gender/age rate closeness does not establish fairness, absence of discrimination, or that information status causally explains all disparity. Many members without declines are still unserved. Information status is associated with access, not an exhaustive causal decomposition.
- Small raw Brier/ECE in a ~1% terminal event problem needs a constant-rate comparator. Pooled calibration across arms/laws is not proof the directional offset helps, and mean calibration can hide bin/subgroup errors. Explain sign conventions: Round 2 signed_bias is prediction minus observation; Round 1's report uses the reverse sign in its displayed number.
- "Oracle drift" supplies the true schedule/offset to one misspecified parametric scorer; it is not a full-information optimal matching oracle. Its outcome cannot bound all adaptive controllers.
