# Extended empirical audit and statistical contract

The shipped simulator/evaluator are the source of truth. `results/extended/audit.json` records the starting audit, including episode rows and exact prior-versus-reproduced comparisons. On seed 101, all six families were independently replayed for greedy, full, and product (18 episodes): assignment counts, mutual acceptances, date counts, qualified MSMI counts, ask costs, and action hashes all matched the archived rows. This validates the starting artifacts, not general superiority.

Archived exploratory seeds 101–105 give full score 0.300 versus greedy 0.3667 MSMI/100. Archived product's fresh-at-the-time seeds 301–305 give +0.1667 over greedy, paired seed SD 0.2041 and a confidence interval containing zero. These findings remain negative/inconclusive; those seeds are now examined and cannot be reused as untouched tests. The seed inventory also finds 201–205 used by selection or offline interventions, 20260926 in scratch simulator work, 42 in graph trials, and public snapshot seeds 20261000–20261009. New development, selection, and confirmation ranges are 1001–1008, 2001–2012, and 3001–3040 respectively; the lead's registry governs those experiments.

A seed, containing its six related scenario-family worlds, is the independent statistical unit. The primary score equally averages the six family scores inside each seed and then averages seed clusters. For a practical +0.10 MSMI/100, pilot SD 0.2041 yields normal-approximation 80% power at two-sided .05 with approximately 33 independent clusters; +0.05 requires approximately 131. Forty confirmation clusters are a defensible budget for the former effect, not a guarantee of power against an unknown candidate variance. The original five-cluster pilot is imprecise. No sample extension is licensed by a p-value. A fixed candidate and predefined greedy comparison must be frozen before confirmation.

`research/extended_analysis.py` checks complete six-family clusters, duplicate rows, invalid episodes, and source completion. It reports Student t intervals, paired t tests, standardized paired effects, a reproducible 10,000-replicate paired seed bootstrap sensitivity interval (random seed 90210), and Holm adjustment of exploratory comparisons. A constant nonzero paired difference has an undefined t test; it is not automatically assigned p=0. Calibration reliability uses a separate whole-seed bootstrap, preserving within-seed dependence. Rare terminal positives make normal approximations and discrimination estimates fragile.

Simulator reconciliation: actions occur on days 0–59 and observation continues to day 100. Twelve percent of members receive one exit day sampled uniformly from 22–60; this is not a 12% daily departure hazard. Eleven hard fields must be observed; declined fields remain permanently unavailable. A member may have several distinct introductions, but the same dyad cannot repeat. Busy release times and future outcomes are latent; observed availability alone does not identify departure. Dates qualify within 30 days after assignment, and positive second intentions within three days after the date. Retirement occurs after both positive second intentions even when qualification deadlines fail. The final feedback of the latest possible delayed introduction occurs by day 97, so all experimental terminal labels have matured at official follow-up.

The harmonic score at p_A=p_B=.5 is .5, while the independent joint probability is .25. It is a ranking score, not joint calibration. A dyad with two incomplete endpoints exposes zero feasible edges under either singleton query and one under the joint query: graph acquisition has complementarity. Improvements inside a disconnected graph can increase matching value while global algebraic connectivity remains zero. Theory proofs live in the separate theory deliverable.

The audit quantifies the forbidden plug-in approximation. Across 1,215 public-logit/equality-prior cases (three regimes, partially missing feature patterns and five fixed actor logits), `sigmoid(E[x])` differs from exact missing-feature enumeration by up to 0.02092 directionally and 0.03098 for reciprocal squared probabilities. In the maximal directional example exact probability is .24362 versus plug-in .22270. This is a numerical counterexample, not a global error bound. In 48 complete-feature/prior-actor cases, nested five-point versus 32-point Gaussian quadrature differs by at most 0.00000356. This supports the tested numerical approximation without certifying all posterior states.

The trace audit consumes assignment-time public snapshots and completed feedback. Pending observations, explicit no-response, rejection, date cancellation, positive but late intentions, and qualified rewards remain distinct. It computes log loss, Brier score, AUROC when both labels occur, scenario and missingness calibration, reciprocal joint calibration, terminal calibration, and seed-bootstrap reliability intervals. Hidden actors and soft truth are used only for an explicitly separate offline oracle probability audit. Summing oracle probabilities on a policy's realized assignments is descriptive quality evidence; it is not an oracle policy score or a causal explanation, because assignments depend on prior outcomes and retirement.

Written review to the algorithm agent identified two potential confounds before freezing: removing the shared effect entirely also removes its marginal variance, so it is not a dependence-only ablation; and a constant control trained on unconditional joint yes labels must not multiply response probability twice. The algorithm agent was asked to preserve marginal variance in the independent comparator and separate conditional from unconditional stage targets. Terminal response-rate uncertainty requires actor second moments because the same latent response propensity governs introduction and second feedback; multiplying four posterior means understates this dependency. These are model requirements, not findings of performance improvement.

Written review to the lead identified that a summary pooling different policies is not a policy's primary score, and that resume keys require configuration/code identity checks. The lead owns the runner corrections. In-process latency measures omit interpreter startup and container isolation; only subprocess and container verification can establish their respective limits. Demographic tables describe synthetic coverage and selected trajectories, not causal discrimination mechanisms.

Reciprocal algorithm review: the simple control's five unconditional funnel predictions are direct mature empirical counts. Its directional acceptance/second keys remain parametric auxiliary diagnostics; they do not factorize the simple score and must not be described as calibrated simple-model components. Parametric response updating resolves explicit events but lacks a pending survival likelihood, so faster feedback can create transient timing bias. Simple final labels mature at age 38. The independent Gaussian comparator preserves marginal Gaussian variance and feature/regime uncertainty; it removes only the common Gaussian effect conditional on those mixture states.

Mechanism replay can recreate historical public observations from the recorded legal actions in an identical simulator world. `--mechanisms --replay-graphs` verifies replayed introductions match the trace and measures verified available nodes, feasible untried edges, local component size, current maximum cardinality, and before-versus-after-query edge expansion. This isolates the direct visibility consequence of queries while holding all other actions and outcomes fixed. It does not identify the causal effect of changing the entire query policy on terminal reward. Largest positive and negative paired seed clusters carry full assignment days, dyads and completed events for failure review. Figures report descriptive trajectory comparisons and cluster uncertainty.

Theory review further distinguishes the marginal-product calibration diagnostic from the policy's Gaussian-independence ablation. Multiplying marginal acceptances and intentions after integrating unknown goal/regime removes mixture dependence as well as common Gaussian dependence. The `marginal_product_reference_calibration` output is explicitly labeled accordingly; `independent_fifo` preserves shared feature/regime mixture states and removes only their conditional common Gaussian effect. The two comparisons answer different questions.

The stronger common-cohort audit, `audit_common_cohort_calibration.json`, replays all 48 greedy development trajectories and scores the same 3,567 prospective assignments under four separate model memories. Each memory receives only the recreated observation state at the ask/match time; snapshots are stored before that assignment's outcomes. Greedy's original asks and pairs are forced, and replay introductions exactly match the recorded trajectory. No model allocates, no model trains across seeds, and final 40-day follow-up supplies labels only after predictions are complete.

On this identical cohort, pooled terminal log loss is .060439 (integrated), .060788 (independent), .061101 (simple), and .060704 (no learning). AUROC is .616, .614, .545, and .576 respectively. Paired comparisons equally average six family mean losses within each seed. Terminal log-loss differences relative to integrated are +.000345 independent (95% CI [-.000713,.001402]), +.000824 simple ([-.001305,.002953]), and +.000274 no learning ([-.000416,.000964]); all global Holm-adjusted p-values are 1.0. This provides no established terminal-prediction superiority, despite integrated's favorable point estimates. Simple's both-response stage is worse than integrated on this cohort: log loss +.009088, CI [.005139,.013037], global Holm p=.0289; Brier +.004427, Holm p=.0296. The multiplicity family contains all 30 stage × proper-score × nonreference-model tests; within-stage three-comparison Holm values are also recorded. A stage-specific exploratory advantage does not establish terminal reward improvement. The paired common-cohort figure was visually verified.

Selected-candidate mechanism audit: simple_fifo and greedy were replayed in all 48 development worlds (96 trajectories), including exact current public feasibility graphs before and after each query. Their ask schedules are identical on every day in every matched world, with mean ask cost 200.375. Therefore clarification improvements do not explain this candidate's development difference. Simple averages 75.479 introductions versus 74.313 greedy, 85.938 repeat endpoint assignments versus 83.667, and 9.436 busy days per assignment versus 9.732. Current verified feasible untried edges average 2.344 versus 2.256, but these reflect different availability and earlier assignments, not a different query schedule. Queries expose 27.188 versus 27.354 currently usable new edges per episode. These are descriptive timing/occupancy differences.

Simple's realized development score is .46875 versus .41667 MSMI/100, five additional qualified outcomes over 48 worlds. However summed oracle conditional probabilities along those realized assignments average .713944 versus .714302 expected qualified counts per episode: no corresponding gain in this conditional quality audit. Simple's mean oracle quality per assigned edge is lower (.009566 versus .009705). It generates 87 both-positive second-intention dyads versus 92 greedy, yet qualifies 45 versus 40. Late feedback disqualifies 42 versus 52 such dyads; greedy also has one late date, overlapping a late-feedback case. The deadline draws are structurally independent of known quality, and the selected policy does not predict them. Favorable realized deadline outcomes are therefore a plausible contributor to this development point gain; this is not evidence of improved deadline prediction or a causal policy-quality improvement.
