## Public-history reduction

At a node, the support is every hidden state consistent with the complete public action/observation history, with exact posterior masses summing to one. Equal hidden states combine by adding masses. Each support state must expose exactly the same admissible action menu. A disagreement raises an error; intersecting menus would hide an information or legality defect.

For a single chosen action, enumerate each supported state's transition branches. A branch contributes prior mass times branch probability. Group successors only by their public observation. The group probability is the sum of branch masses; dividing each branch mass by that probability gives the next posterior. Actions are chosen **after** this grouping. Branch reward is booked in expectation and is not automatically added to the public observation. All histories retain their own previous actions and observations. There is no first-hidden-state cache or cache indexed only by the latest observation.

Induction on the finite remaining phases proves each action's value is its expected once-booked reward plus observation-probability-weighted successor value. The maximum over the common legal menu is attainable by a public-history policy. Conversely every legal public-history policy picks a common-menu action and is bounded by that maximum. Terminal value is zero. Exact comparisons preserve all ties. This proves optimality for the declared finite models, subject to independent review of implementation and model definitions; it does not establish simulator-model fidelity.

The constructed public observation contains current availability, answered/failed bundles of actors who have arrived, used dyads, explicitly released reduced-model events, and pending identities. Private busy-until timers, hidden actor answer types, future arrival realizations, pause flags, and pending expectation values are absent. Availability may reveal a return when its day is reached. Internal full-state traces are audit evidence and are never exposed as decision inputs.
