# Primary literature and novelty boundary

9 October2026. Researcher searched primary author/proceedings sources using web tools. This is an applicability audit, not a systematic review or exhaustive novelty claim. No model source was changed following screening start.

[Gneiting and Raftery, Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation (2007)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) defines probability scores that reward honest predictive distributions. Log loss and Brier in this round are inherited proper-score tools. Their utility is forecast assessment; those scores alone do not optimize the terminal reward of a policy whose assignments change occupancy and future options. Simulator-specific evidence must separately test calibration and policy reward.

[Elmachtoub and Grigas, Smart Predict, then Optimize (author preprint, v5)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08005) explicitly makes downstream optimization structure part of prediction training through decision error and a convex surrogate. Decision-focused learning is established, so this round's separation of predictive and policy value is not a novel learning framework. The paper's surrogate consistency concerns specified optimization problems/conditions; this simulator's changing graph, partial observations and delayed retirement are additional sequential complications. This round does not implement SPO+ or inherit its guarantees.

[Koller and Sahami, Toward Optimal Feature Selection (ICML1996, author PDF)](https://ai.stanford.edu/~koller/Papers/Koller%2BSahami%3AICML96.pdf) is a close antecedent for removing irrelevant/redundant information. Author catalog metadata and the author-hosted PDF were located; automated PDF extraction was garbled, so no detailed theorem from that PDF is newly verified here. This round does not learn a Markov blanket. Instead, public simulator code directly shows three soft fields absent from the transition probability and hard eligibility expressions. Conditional on fixed other inputs they cannot alter the current edge outcome law. That structural source audit is specific to this simulator; recommending removal of irrelevant predictors is an established idea. Dynamic policy improvement still requires an experiment, since changing ranks alters subsequent states.

[Akbarpour, Li and Oveis Gharan, Thickness and Information in Dynamic Matching Markets (JPE2020, author manuscript)](https://web.stanford.edu/~mohamwad/DynamicMatching.pdf) links waiting benefits to the information available about departures. Its model has stochastic arrivals/departures and persistent compatibility links, with matched agents leaving the market. This provides the closest dynamic-network motivation for auditing scarcity and waiting. Here assignments often fail, endpoints return, hard information costs budget, and imminent exits are unavailable; therefore its near-optimality statements for greedy/local timing do not transfer.

[Baccara, Lee and Yariv, Optimal Dynamic Matching (Theoretical Economics2020; author draft19August2019)](https://www.lyariv.com/papers/DynamicMatching.pdf) studies the tradeoff between match quality and waiting costs, using a structured two-type/supermodular setting. Its optimal threshold/priority findings motivate tiny waiting counterexamples but do not establish the correct reservation rule in a general reciprocal graph with repeated attempts and hidden outcome parameters. No threshold guarantee is claimed in this round.

[Pizzato et al., RECON: A Reciprocal Recommender for Online Dating (RecSys2010, primary paper)](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=1eba75ecbb4878d419298fb77f585b6e8955e8a4&repid=rep1&type=pdf) and [Xia, Liu, Sun and Chen, Reciprocal Recommendation System for Online Dating (author preprint2015)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06247) establish the use of preferences on both sides and reciprocal recommendation scores. Those established content/collaborative methods motivate reciprocal scoring, not a proof that an aggregation score is a calibrated joint probability or that top-ranked current introductions maximize final qualified second intentions. Our prior/common-effect integration and observation-only history audit address this simulator's particular joint funnel.

The honest contribution claim, if evidence supports it, is a reproducible simulator benchmark separating irrelevant feature ranks, probability learning, cardinality and occupancy under delayed reciprocal outcomes. The exact tiny counterexamples illustrate standard tradeoffs rather than proving a new full-simulator optimum. Public-mechanism integration, ridge logistic fitting, strong actor shrinkage, degree priorities and proper-score calibration are inherited methods. Broad novelty remains uncertain. Generalization requires checking the information boundary, no-repeat dyads, actor/effect persistence, delay distributions and endpoint retirement; those assumptions are neither established nor proposed as facts about real dating markets.
